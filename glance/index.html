Tonight's games. What you've got riding on them.

A small Mac app that lists your open Kalshi sports positions by game: the score, what you paid, what each side is worth right now, and what it pays if it hits. One window, or one number in the menu bar.

What it's for

Keep tabs without keeping a tab open.

Kalshi's portfolio page updates live too. It also needs a browser tab, a login, and the rest of the site around it. This is the same feed in a plain window you can leave open, or a number in the menu bar, without the browser.

No Spex server, ever. The app keeps a cache of your latest numbers on your Mac so it opens instantly, and Disconnect clears it. Live Line, off by default, keeps your P&L history encrypted on your Mac or in your own iCloud. Your API key stays in the Keychain (and in your iCloud Keychain only if you turn on sharing).

Up close. Cost, what it's worth now, what it pays, chance, and the running P&L on each side.

Features

What it does, and not much else.

01Live from your own Kalshi account

Your own read-only API key, straight to Kalshi. One connection carries prices, fills, orders and results, so a change on Kalshi shows up in the window within a second or two. A five-minute refresh runs behind it as a safety net, and the live dot goes amber if the feed falls behind.

02Connection, in the menu bar

If the app can't get your numbers, the icon turns ember or red and the dropdown says why: your network, your VPN, your key, or requests failing. The numbers on screen stay put, marked stale. Spex Glance doesn't check Kalshi's service status; Kalshi's Developer Agreement doesn't allow apps to monitor it.

03Work Mode

⇧⌘M hides the icon, the dollar sign, the money tiles and the buttons. What's left on screen is a list of games. Sharing your display stops being a disclosure.

04Filter by sport, sort by match time

Baseball, hockey, tennis, and whatever else Kalshi tags as sports. One click to see only tonight's slate. Sort by Live first, earliest match, or latest, so what's on now sits at the top and tomorrow's early tennis waits its turn.

05Combos, leg by leg

A multi-market combo shows as one card with every leg listed by sport and game, each leg's chance, and a check or cross as they settle. No mental math about which combo is still alive.

06Read-only. Always.

It runs on a read-only Kalshi key and has no order code. It can't place, change or cancel an order, and it won't accept a key that could.

What it isn't

It can't place an order. That's the point.

Sports positions only. Anything else you hold is counted in the totals, not listed. It relays Kalshi's numbers and has no opinion about them.

Free, open source, MIT. Source on GitHub, optional donations on Ko-fi, no account, no analytics, no crash reports, no install counting.

Setup

Three steps. About five minutes.

Kalshi has no "sign in with Kalshi" for third-party apps, so connecting is a one-time manual step on kalshi.com. The app walks you through it.

  1. Download and open Spex GlanceGrab the latest release from GitHub, or brew install --cask spexbet/tap/spex-glance. It's notarized, so macOS opens it like any other app.
  2. Create a read-only API key on KalshiThe connect wizard points you at the right page on kalshi.com. Create a key with read-only permissions and drop the key file into the app.
  3. GlanceYour open sports positions show up grouped by game. Turn on the menu bar item if you want it, and get back to whatever you were doing.

FAQ

Questions people ask before installing a thing that touches their money.

Is this affiliated with Kalshi?

No. Spex Glance is an independent open-source project. It's not affiliated with, endorsed by, or supported by Kalshi. "Kalshi" is their trademark.

Where does my key go?

Into your macOS Keychain, on your Mac. The app talks directly to Kalshi's API. There's no Spex server in between. If you turn on key sharing in Settings (off by default; it's for early testing of the iPhone app, as an easier setup), a copy also goes into your iCloud Keychain, end-to-end encrypted by Apple, so Spex Glance on your other Apple devices signed in to the same Apple Account can use it. Turn it off and the copy is deleted.

Are the numbers real time?

As current as Kalshi's ticker sends them. They can lag or be wrong. Confirm on Kalshi before acting on anything.

Does it collect anything?

Nothing. No analytics, no crash reports, no install counting. The app makes network calls to Kalshi's API for your positions, to GitHub once a day to check for updates, and to your own iCloud only if you turn on Live Line sync or key sharing. GitHub sees an IP address and the app version on that check, the same as any app that updates itself. Details in the privacy note.

Does Kalshi have a Mac app or desktop app?

Kalshi's iPhone app runs on Apple silicon Macs. Spex Glance is a separate, independent, read-only Mac app that shows only your Kalshi sports positions, in a window and the menu bar, using Kalshi's public API. It isn't made by Kalshi.

Does it work with Polymarket, FanDuel, DraftKings, or other sportsbooks?

No. Kalshi only. Spex Glance reads positions through Kalshi's API, and the other platforms have different APIs, different account models, and in the sportsbooks' case no public read API at all. Nothing planned there.

Is there an iPhone version?

Yes, in private testing: Spex Glance for iPhone, with an Apple Watch app and widgets. It's invite-only on TestFlight for now and not on the App Store yet.

Can I get the intimate details of the inner workings of this?

Yes. All of it is on GitHub: the Swift source, the build scripts, the release process, and the README that explains how the app talks to Kalshi. MIT licensed, so you can read it, build it yourself, or fork it.

Why is it called Spex?

A haruspex was a Roman priest who predicted the future by reading the entrails of a sacrificed animal. That's roughly what checking your Kalshi portfolio at 11pm feels like. Spex is the tail end of that word, and also specs, as in the glasses in the logo. Less mess, same anxiety.
