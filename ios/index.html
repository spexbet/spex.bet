Live games

Tap a live game. Watch it from there.

A collapsed card is one line per position: what you hold and its chance, green when it's worth more than you paid. Open a live game and you get the scoreboard straight from Kalshi's live feed: score, period or inning, who has the ball or the serve, runners and count, the last play. Under it, each position's value, payout and cost.

  • Prices stream live while the app is open; scores refresh every 15 seconds.
  • Games sorted into Live now, Upcoming, and Final awaiting settlement.
  • A P&L tab with your realized results by day, week, month or all time, and by sport.
  • Work Mode hides every dollar amount when the phone is on a desk.

Follow on Lock Screen

Keep the game on screen without opening the app.

Tap Follow on Lock Screen on a live game and it sits on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island: the score, the inning or quarter, your side's chance, and the game's P&L. It also shows up in your Apple Watch's Smart Stack.

Honest about freshness: there's no Spex server pushing updates, so the app updates it itself while it's open and whenever iOS lets it refresh in the background. When it can't, it dims and says when the numbers are from instead of passing them off as live. It ends on its own once Kalshi decides the market.

Widgets

Your positions on the home screen and the Lock Screen.

The Mac's widgets, on your iPhone: a cobalt tile with unrealized P&L, games held and how many are live; a list of your top games with chances and P&L; and a large size with your balances and resting orders. On the Lock Screen: P&L in a line, in a circle, or your top game with its score.

Every widget says when its numbers are from. iOS refreshes widgets on its own schedule, so they're never truly live; the app nudges them whenever it has new numbers.

Apple Watch

Glance at your wrist.

Spex Glance comes with an Apple Watch app and watch-face complications. The watch holds no Kalshi key and never talks to Kalshi: your iPhone sends it the numbers. Pick a complication when you edit your watch face: Unrealized P&L, Top game (score and your chance), or Balances. Dollar amounts hide while the watch is locked, and one switch keeps them off entirely.

Same rules as the Mac

Read-only

Runs on a read-only Kalshi API key and has no order code. It checks the key when you connect and won't accept one that could trade.

Straight to Kalshi

No Spex server, no account. The key stays in your iPhone's Keychain. The watch gets numbers from the phone, never the key.

Easy setup

Create a read-only key on Kalshi and open its file in Spex Glance. Already on the Mac app? Turn on key sharing there (optional, off by default) and the iPhone connects in one tap.

Common questions
Can I download it?

Not yet. Spex Glance for iPhone and Apple Watch is in private testing through Apple's TestFlight, invite-only. It isn't on the App Store and there's no public sign-up yet. Check back here.

Do I need the Mac app?

No. The iPhone app connects to Kalshi on its own with a read-only API key. If you already use Spex Glance on a Mac, you can turn on key sharing there (Settings, Kalshi key) so the iPhone connects without making a second key. It's optional and off by default; the copy travels in your iCloud Keychain, which Apple encrypts end to end.

Does the watch need my Kalshi key?

No. The watch never talks to Kalshi and holds no key. Your iPhone sends it the numbers and game lines it shows. The watch's complications are as fresh as the iPhone's last update and say when they're from.

Can it place an order?

No. Like every Spex app it runs on a read-only Kalshi key and has no order code. It won't accept a key that could trade.

Is it open source?

Yes. The iPhone and watch apps live in the same spex-glance repository as the Mac app, MIT licensed.
