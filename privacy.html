Privacy

This covers Spex Glance (Mac, and iPhone and Apple Watch in private testing) and the spex.bet site. Short version: Spex Glance runs on your devices, gets your data only from Kalshi, and has no server of its own.

Your Kalshi API key

Stored in your device's Keychain. Used only to call Kalshi's API from your device. The app requires a read-only key and cannot place, amend, or cancel orders.

Optional, off by default, for early testing of Spex Glance for iPhone as an easier setup: Settings → Kalshi key → Share puts a copy of the key in your iCloud Keychain, which Apple encrypts end to end, so Spex Glance on your other Apple devices signed in to the same Apple Account can connect without a new key. Only Spex Glance can read that copy. Turning sharing off deletes it. Apart from this, the key is never sent anywhere but Kalshi.

Your positions and balances

Fetched from Kalshi and shown on screen. The app keeps a cache of your latest positions and settled positions on your device so it opens quickly; Disconnect clears it. Not logged, not uploaded to Spex, not shared.

Live Line (Mac, optional)

Off by default. If you turn it on, the Mac app keeps a history of your sports total for the P&L chart. You choose where: on this Mac, encrypted with a key that stays in this Mac's Keychain, or in your own private iCloud database, which only your Apple Account can read. Turning it off stops recording; Clear history deletes it.

Kalshi's service status

Spex Glance doesn't monitor Kalshi's service status. Kalshi's Developer Agreement (§3.5) doesn't allow apps to use the API to monitor the availability or performance of Kalshi's services, so the app only reports its own connection.

iPhone and Apple Watch (private testing)

The same rules as the Mac. The key stays in your iPhone's Keychain and is used only to call Kalshi's API; key sharing with your Mac works the same way as above. The iPhone sends your Apple Watch the numbers and game lines it shows, over Apple's own connection between the two; the watch holds no key and never talks to Kalshi. While in testing, Apple's TestFlight may collect crash reports and feedback you choose to send; that goes to Apple and the Spex developer account, under Apple's terms.

What is collected

Nothing. There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no install counting, and no third-party SDK in the app other than Sparkle, which only checks for updates.

Network calls the app makes

Up to three. It calls Kalshi's API to fetch your positions and prices, under Kalshi's terms. If you turn on Live Line sync or key sharing, it talks to your own iCloud through Apple. Once a day it fetches a small update manifest from GitHub (Sparkle, the standard Mac update framework). GitHub sees your IP address and a User-Agent string with the app and macOS version on that request, the same as any self-updating Mac app. You can turn off automatic update checks in Settings.

This website

Static pages hosted on GitHub Pages. No analytics script, no cookies set by the site. GitHub logs the visitor's IP address for security purposes, per its documentation. See GitHub's privacy statement.

Third parties

Kalshi (their API, under their terms), GitHub (downloads, update checks, and this site), Apple (iCloud, only if you turn on Live Line sync or key sharing; TestFlight while the iPhone app is in testing), Ko-fi (only if you click the donation link on this site).
