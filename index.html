Spex Glance for Mac

Where you stand on tonight's games, in one window.

On Kalshi, checking a night of sports bets means paging through open positions for the current odds and tapping into each closed order to see how it ended. Spex Glance puts every open sports position on one screen, grouped by game. Each bet shows its current chance and what it pays, then Won or Lost once the game ends.

The gap

Placing a bet on Kalshi is quick. Checking on it isn't.

Kalshi's portfolio covers every market you trade, sports or not, and puts the outcome of a bet one tap behind its order. That's fine for managing bets. It's slow when all you want to know is whether tonight is going well.

Checking on Kalshi

Dig, tap, repeat.

  • Open your portfolio and scroll for tonight's games among everything else you hold.
  • Dig through your open orders to see where each bet stands right now.
  • Tap into each closed order, one at a time, to find out whether it won.
  • Add it up in your head.
Checking in Spex Glance

Look once.

  • Every open sports position on one screen, grouped by game.
  • Chance, cost and payout on every line, with a live clock once the game starts.
  • Won or Lost on the card when the game ends. Finished bets move to the Settled tab with the day's total.
  • Cash, Positions, Total and unrealized P&L at the top, and a live P&L number in the menu bar.
What it does, and what it doesn't

A scoreboard for your bets. Kalshi does the rest.

Glance only reads. Anything that changes your account still happens on Kalshi, and the Open Kalshi portfolio button takes you there.

What Glance does

Keeps tabs.

  • Shows your active sports positions: upcoming, live, and final until Kalshi settles them.
  • Groups them by game, with the chance, cost and payout on each bet.
  • Lists finished bets on the Settled tab with Won or Lost and each day's total.
  • Keeps a live P&L number in the menu bar.
  • Counts non-sports positions in your total without listing them.
What Glance can't do

Head to Kalshi for these.

  • Place, change or cancel a bet.
  • Show markets you don't hold. It has no search or browsing.
  • Move money or change account settings.
  • List politics, weather or other non-sports positions.
  • Create its own API key. That's a one-time step on kalshi.com.

The rules, for every Spex app

Read-only, always

Each app runs on a read-only Kalshi API key and has no order code. Nothing under the Spex name places, amends, or cancels a bet.

Your data stays yours

Apps talk straight to Kalshi. No Spex server in the middle, no account to create, nothing about your positions leaves your devices.

Common questions
Is Spex affiliated with Kalshi?

No. Spex is an independent open-source project. It isn't affiliated with, endorsed by, or supported by Kalshi. "Kalshi" is their trademark.

Does Spex Glance replace the Kalshi app?

No. Kalshi is where you place, change and cancel bets. Spex Glance only shows you where your sports positions stand, and its Open Kalshi portfolio button takes you back there when you want to act.

Does Spex work with Polymarket, FanDuel, DraftKings, or other sportsbooks?

No. Every Spex app reads from Kalshi's API and nothing else. Other prediction markets and sportsbooks have their own APIs or none at all, and there's no plan to add them.

Can any Spex app place a bet for me?

No. They run on read-only Kalshi API keys and have no order code. They show you what you hold and what it's worth.

Why is it called Spex?

A haruspex was a Roman priest who predicted the future by reading the entrails of a sacrificed animal. That's roughly what checking your Kalshi portfolio at 11pm feels like. Spex is the tail end of that word, and also specs, as in the glasses in the logo. Less mess, same anxiety.

Everything under the Spex name is free and stays that way. Tips cover the Apple developer account that keeps the Mac app signed and notarized.
